# 03 — Problem Framing

## The problem

Neighborhood shopkeepers (*tenderos*) in Neiva manage inventory and sales manually using
paper notebooks, memory, and physical calculators. Stock counts are inaccurate, perishable
goods expire unnoticed on shelves, and checkout queues slow down during peak hours because
cashiers must recall prices by memory.

## Who is affected

| Group | How |
|---|---|
| Tendero (Shop Owner) | Loses capital from spoiled perishables; makes emergency trips to Surabasto; lacks daily net profit numbers |
| Cajero (Cashier) | Experiences arithmetic errors during rush hours; enters into price disputes with customers |
| Barrio Customers | Suffer long checkout lines; find staple goods out of stock without warning |
| Platform SuperAdmin | Struggles to support non-technical merchants without an integrated helpdesk |

## Cost of doing nothing

A typical neighborhood store in Neiva loses between 5% and 12% of its working capital every
month to expired goods and untracked merchandise shrinkage. When high-demand staples run out,
customers walk to competing stores. Every evening, the tendero spends over an hour manually
reconciling paper receipts to calculate daily revenue.

## Success criterion

The problem is solved when:
1. A sale is scanned, rung up, and stock deducted in under 15 seconds.
2. 100% of products expiring within 15 days are surfaced on the dashboard before spoiling.
3. Daily cash close and net profit are calculated automatically at close of business.
4. Non-technical shopkeepers can restock and query inventory via natural voice commands.
